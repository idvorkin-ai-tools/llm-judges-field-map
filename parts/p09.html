When not to trust a judge

A judge is validated for a policy, a population of items and a use. Step outside any of them and the validation stops covering you. Every older field keeps a list of the cases that go to someone else.
TL;DR — Seven situations break a judge. Three mean don't use the grade until you fix the cause: humans don't agree on the policy; the judge has a stake (its own model family, or a system tuned against it); the items are unlike the gold set. Four mean use it only as a flag for a second look: the failure is rare, so most flags are false; one item must be decided rather than five hundred averaged; the judged text carries instructions for the judge; the reasons contradict the score. The older fields' remedies are the same everywhere: a second reader, a panel, an appeal, a committee.

Units 04 to 08 built a judge you can measure; this unit is where the measurement stops. Kappa 0.6 on last month's gold set is earned on that set, for that policy, as an average. It does not transfer by default to a new product surface, to a model trained against the judge, or to the one case someone will be fired, rejected or sued over.

Every institution that judges at scale has written down when the first call is final. Split candidates go to a debrief or hiring committee, not one interviewer.Every rating passes a calibration meeting; career-moving ones get the most scrutiny.Judges sit in panels, so no single palate decides a medal.European guidelines recommend reading every mammogram twice, with a third reader or consensus for disagreements.Managers can challenge calls on video, and since 2026 players can challenge balls and strikes against tracking. Each rule is a decision about when not to trust the judge in front of you.

The decision in one picture

The order matters. The first three questions ask whether your validation applies at all; if not, the numbers from unit 08 describe some other situation. The last two assume it holds and ask whether this use can live with the judge's error rate. A judge 83.5% accurate is fine for some jobs and dangerous for others.

When there is nothing to check against

(a) The humans do not agree

A judge is validated against people. If two trained labelers agree at kappa 0.49, as in unit 04, that is a ceiling: with humans the only yardstick, the model can at most match them. Parity at a low number is still a low number. Zheng et al. 2023 report GPT-4 at over 80% agreement with human preferences, “the same level of agreement between humans”: good news because the human level was high.

Low agreement means fix the policy, not the judge: people disagreeing a quarter of the time are telling you the rubric leaves room for taste. Igor's recipe puts consistent human application before automation; otherwise you get a fast, cheap, confident version of the disagreement. And mind the kappa paradox: with rare failures, 92% raw agreement can sit at kappa 0.29.

Two interviewers, one candidate

Ana and Ben split on a quarter of candidates; a model imitating either inherits the split. The fix comes first: anchored answers on the scorecard, so both score the same thing.

Two managers, one employee

Carla and Dev rate the same work “exceeds” and “meets”, and no outside truth settles it. No judge can be validated until the definitions make informed managers converge.

Nothing beneath the panel

Wine has no outside truth, and judges do not even agree with themselves. In Hodgson's replicate pours, only about 10 percent kept one wine within a single medal group across three blind pours; another 10 percent scored the same wine anywhere from Bronze to Gold (Hodgson 2008). Matching them is matching noise.

Ten readers, the same films

Ten radiologists reading the same 150 mammograms recommended work-up for 11 to 65 percent of the women without cancer; pairs agreed at weighted kappa 0.47 (Elmore et al. 1994). Medicine is lucky: biopsy and follow-up let a judge be checked against outcomes, not readers.

The one domain that escapes

Ivo and June disagree on borderline pitches too, but tracking measures where the ball crossed and the rulebook defines the zone. Human disagreement caps validation only where people are the truth; behind the plate, the camera is.

(c) The items are off the map

A judge's numbers hold for items like those it was validated on. Change the task, language, product surface, users or the model producing the outputs, and they are a hope. Every test fails this way: a reader's screening-population sensitivity does not carry to a symptomatic clinic. LLM judges have it worse: outputs shift whenever the system under test changes, so the gold set ages fast, and people's criteria move once they see new outputs (Shankar et al. 2024, criteria drift).

The remedy is cheap: label 50 to 100 fresh items and re-run the unit 08 checks. Until then, the grade on a new kind of rolea new kind of joban unfamiliar stylea new kind of imagea new league's zone is an untested instrument.

The scorecard for a different job

An interviewer calibrated on hundreds of backend engineers interviews a designer. The scorecard still yields numbers; nobody knows what they mean for a year.

A rating for a role nobody has held

A manager rates the first person in a new role, against expectations written for other jobs, with no past cases to compare. The rating drafts the standard rather than applying it.

A judge outside their region

A reliable palate for one region's reds, seated on a style it rarely tastes, may score consistently and still miss that style's typicity. Competitions seat judges by category.

Same reader, different clinic

A reader measured on routine screening films moves to a symptomatic clinic or a new scanner. Case mix and images change, and so do the numbers; re-audit first.

A zone that moved

A league with a different zone, or a new tracking setup, changes what “correct” means; accuracy must be measured again.

When the judge has a stake, or the text fights back

(b) The judge grades its own side

A judge stops being independent in two ways. Directly: it grades its own model family. Panickssery et al. 2024 found models recognise their own text better than chance, and the better they recognise it, the stronger their self-preference: they score it above what human raters give. “Our model versus theirs” graded by our model's sibling has the bias built in (unit 07).

Indirectly, and more often: the system was optimised against the judge. Use a judge as a reward signal, a best-of-n picker or a hill-climbing target, and the system learns whatever it rewards, errors included. Gao, Schulman and Hilton 2022 measured it for reward models: push hard enough on an imperfect proxy and true quality stops rising, then falls.

When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.— Marilyn Strathern's phrasing of Goodhart's law, Strathern 1997

So validation goes stale the moment you tune against the judge: the gold set predates outputs that learned its blind spots. Re-validate on fresh outputs, and keep a judge or a set of human labels that the optimisation never sees.

Candidates who learned the questions

Once a question bank leaks, candidates rehearse; the scorecard still says “hire” but measures less. An interviewer grading their own referral is the self-preference case.

Work shaped to the rating sheet

Reward visible launches and people produce visible launches; ratings rise whether or not the work improved. A manager rating a project they championed has a stake.

A wine made for the medal

A producer can make a wine for the panel rather than the table. Scores rise; the link between medal and what drinkers value weakens. Goodhart in a glass.

Reading your own prior report

A radiologist re-reviewing their own case anchors on their earlier call, so audits and second reads use a reader who has not seen the first report.

Framing the pitch

Catchers frame borderline pitches to look like strikes: the pitch has not moved, the judge's perception has been gamed. The camera does not see the glove move.

(f) The judged text talks to the judge

A person can tell a document from instructions for reading it; a language model cannot reliably. Untrusted text, from a user, another model, a scraped page or an adversary, can carry instructions aimed at the judge, and the judge may obey: prompt injection. Raina, Liusie and Gales 2024 found short universal phrases that inflate absolute scores when appended to any text, even on judges the attacker never saw; comparative judging was less exposed. Shi et al. 2024 optimised injected text that makes a pairwise judge pick the attacker, past standard detection defences. Zhao et al. 2025 got false rewards from reference-based judges with a lone punctuation mark or “Thought process:”.

So if the author benefits from a good grade, the grade is a flag. Delimit the text, tell the judge it is data, compare against a reference where you can, and send suspiciously high scores to a human. In the older fields this is the candidate who stuffs every answer with the scorecard's keywordsself-assessment written in the exact words of the next level's definitionwine doctored to mask a fault before it reaches the panelimage artefact that mimics or hides a lesioncatcher pulling the glove back into the zone: the thing being judged is working on the judge.

When one call matters

(d) The failure is rare and costly

Take the unit 05 judge: sensitivity 0.80, specificity 0.85. Where 30% of items are truly YES, its YES calls are right 69.6% of the time. Point it at a failure in 5% of items (a safety violation, a fabricated citation): of 1,000 items it catches 40 of 50 real cases and flags 142.5 clean ones. Precision falls to 0.219; nearly four flags in five are false. The judge did not change; the base rate did.

Try thisKeep the judge fixed and drag prevalence from 30% down to 5% and 1%: the YES pile fills with wrong calls while Youden's J never moves.

Medicine solved this with screening logic. A screening test is not a diagnosis; it decides who gets the expensive, accurate second test, so it is tuned for high sensitivity and accepts false alarms the second test will clear. Use a judge hunting rare, costly failures the same way: its flag buys a second look, never a verdict. Report precision at the real base rate, not on a balanced gold set, and budget reviewers first: at 5% prevalence, 1,000 items produce about 180 flags.

The rare dealbreaker

A reference check flagging possible dishonesty is a screen; most flags are innocent. It triggers a conversation, not a rejection.

The misconduct flag

A hint of misconduct is rare and serious, so it goes to a separate process with its own evidence standard. The manager's impression starts an investigation; it does not end one.

Is it corked?

Cork taint is rare, and a judge who suspects it asks for a second bottle rather than marking down. It is the one wine question a lab can settle, so suspicion is a reason to check.

Screening, by design

A recall is not a diagnosis; it sends the patient to more imaging and maybe biopsy. The screen is tuned to catch, the next steps to clear.

The game-deciding call

A game-ending call is rare and costly. Baseball does not ask the umpire to try harder; it gives teams a challenge checked against tracking.

(e) One case versus five hundred

Errors behave differently in an average than in one decision. On one item the casebook judge is wrong 16.5% of the time, and its YES calls about 30%: not enough to decide anything important. Over many items its errors become a predictable distortion. If it errs the same way on two systems, its measured pass rate is a straight line in the true one: 0.15 + 0.65 × true rate. Gaps shrink to 65% of their size (65% is Youden's J) but keep their sign and order, and the Rogan–Gladen correction recovers the true rate.

So comparing two prompts or models over a few hundred items is what judges do best; blocking one user's message, grading one student or rejecting one contractor is a much harder claim. For the aggregate, report a confidence interval on the difference (Igor's report the noise). At 500 items per system, a 10-point true gap measures 6.5 points, about two standard errors: real, just. Smaller gaps need more items. For the single case, use the judge as triage.

Try thisShrink the true gap or the item count until the measured gap sinks below two standard errors, then weaken the judge and watch the gap shrink by J while Rogan–Gladen still recovers the true rates.

Why loops have several interviewers

No serious process lets one interview decide; the decision is made over several interviewers' notes. One score is an input, as a judge's should be.

The distribution and the person

A committee can defend “this team's forty ratings run high”. Deciding one person is “meets”, not “exceeds”, takes a discussion of their work.

A gold medal is a single item

Across 13 U.S. competitions, of 2,440 wines entered in more than three, 47 percent won a Gold somewhere, and 84 percent of those also won nothing elsewhere (Hodgson 2009). One medal is one judgement.

Programme audits versus patients

Programmes are audited on recall and detection rates over thousands of reads. For one patient, the read starts a pathway that ends in biopsy or follow-up.

Season accuracy, single pitch

A season's evidence can make an umpire excellent and still say nothing about the 3–2 pitch in the ninth, which is why that pitch can be challenged.

When the reasons and the score disagree

Most judge prompts ask for a rationale before the score, which lets a person audit the call and gives you a check: does the score follow from the reasons? Two serious defects then 5/5, or praise then “fail”, means one of the two is wrong and the item alone cannot tell you which. Flag it.

Agreement is not proof either. Turpin et al. 2023 showed chain-of-thought explanations can misstate why a model answered: models nudged toward an answer gave plausible reasons for it and never mentioned the nudge. A stated reason is evidence about the verdict, not a transcript of how it was reached. Read a sample of mismatches, and have a second model or a simple rule score the rationale alone, then compare.

The older fields know this one. Glowing notes under “no hire” get questioned in the debrief: something unwritten drove the call.“Exceeds” work described under a “meets” rating is the first thing a committee asks about.Praise-filled notes beside a low score make the chair ask for a re-taste.A suspicious finding under a benign BI-RADS category is exactly what quality review exists to catch.A ruling explained with the wrong rule gets reviewed by the crew chief, whatever the call.

What the older fields do instead

Every field's remedy: a second, independent look, and a disagreement rule fixed in advance.

The debrief and the committee

Written feedback is collected before the debrief so interviewers do not anchor on each other, and split decisions go to people who were not in the room: double reading plus arbitration.

The calibration meeting

Peers compare proposed ratings across teams and move the ones out of line. With no outside truth, the committee is the arbiter, and it turns opinions into a shared policy.

Panels, not palates

Hodgson's panels had four expert judges each, yet only about half awarded medals on wine quality alone (Hodgson 2008). A jury averages away independent noise, not a shared blind spot.

Double reading, with arbitration

The European Commission Initiative on Breast Cancer suggests double reading with consensus or arbitration over single reading (conditional, moderate certainty; ECIBC). With arbitration it raised detection and lowered recalls in a meta-analysis (Taylor and Potts 2008).

The challenge system

Managers have challenged calls on replay since 2014; from 2026 players can challenge balls and strikes against the Automated Ball-Strike system, keeping a challenge that succeeds. About 54% of 2026's 10,557 challenges overturned the call (Automated Ball-Strike System). The human calls every pitch; the machine is the appeal.

LLM research found juries independently. Verga et al. 2024, “Replacing Judges with Juries”, swapped one large judge for a panel of smaller models from disjoint families: across three settings and six datasets it did better, showed less family bias and cost over seven times less. The family split is the point the wine data makes: three copies of one model are one judge asked three times, with correlated errors. Diversity makes a jury.

Two more rules carry over. Fix the disagreement rule before seeing which way it cuts. And send arbitrated cases back into the gold set: where judges split is where the policy is least clear. Igor's red-pen review loop is the habit at its smallest: a human reads real output before automation is trusted.

The trust checklist

Run this before relying on a grade. A “no” in the first block: don't use the number yet. In the second: flag only.

Carry this away. Validation covers one policy, one kind of item, one use. Without human agreement, with a judge that has a stake, or on new items, fix the cause first. With rare failures, single decisions, hostile text or contradictory reasons, the grade is a flag. When it mattered, the older fields never let one judge decide alone: a second reader, a rule for disagreements, an appeal.

What you can say now

  • A judge cannot be shown to beat the humans it is validated against; low human kappa means fix the policy.
  • Self-preference and optimisation pressure break independence; tune against a judge and its validation is stale.
  • Validation holds for items like the gold set; new task, language or surface needs fresh labels.
  • Sensitivity 0.80 and specificity 0.85 give precision 0.696 at 30% prevalence and 0.219 at 5%: for rare failures, a screen.
  • Over 500 items a judge shrinks a gap without flipping it, so it can compare systems; it is unfit to decide one case.
  • Judged text can attack the judge; untrusted content is flag-only.
  • Double reading with arbitration, diverse panels, appeals and committees are the remedies; one model family is not a jury.
